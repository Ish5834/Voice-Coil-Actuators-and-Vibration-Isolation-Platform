The basic principle for the working of an LVDT (Linear Variable Differential Transformer) is electromagnetic induction. It involves a primary coil, two secondary coils, and a ferromagnetic core. The primary coil is given AC current, which generates an alternating magnetic field around it. This field induces voltages in the two secondary coils on either side of the primary through mutual induction. The two secondary coils are wound in opposition to each other (series-opposing configuration), and hence their output voltages subtract. When the ferromagnetic core is exactly centered, the induced voltages in both secondaries are equal and cancel out, giving zero net output. Either the core or the coil assembly is held stationary, and hence the free one is free to move along the axial direction. In this project, the coil assembly is held stationary (fixed to the housing/leg structure) and hence the core moves with the piston. As the core shifts from center, the magnetic coupling becomes stronger on one side and weaker on the other — the difference in the two secondary voltages gives a signal whose magnitude tells you how far the core has moved, and whose phase tells you which direction.
